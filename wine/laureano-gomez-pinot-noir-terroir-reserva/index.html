About this wine

Laureano Gomez is a small family winery in Tunuyán, in the Uco Valley, run by a winemaker who spent more than two decades making wine at two well-known Argentine wineries before starting this project in 2010. He buys grapes from grower friends across the Uco Valley and works on a deliberately small, hands-on scale: fermentation in stainless steel tanks, ageing in French oak barrels and then time in bottle before release. His Pinot Noir from Tunuyán aims for precision and balance rather than power, with ripe strawberry and tart cherry, an earthy, savoury touch and oak that adds structure without hiding the fruit.

What to eat with it

  1. Pisca hasa. Light body and fresh acidity cut through crisp fried fish, while the red fruit keeps it lively.
    Recipe idea: Season snapper or grouper with garlic, lime and salt, dust in flour and pan-fry until crisp; serve with a quick creole sauce of onion and sweet pepper.
  2. Galinja stoba. Tart cherry and soft tannins suit tender chicken braised with tomato and sweet pepper.
    Recipe idea: Brown chicken pieces, add onion, garlic, tomato, sweet pepper and stock; simmer 45 minutes until tender. Serve with white rice.
  3. Goat cheese and caramelised onion tart. Bright acidity balances creamy goat cheese and the sweetness of slow-cooked onions.
    Recipe idea: Spread caramelised onions on puff pastry, crumble goat cheese over, add thyme and bake at 200 °C for 20 minutes until golden.
  4. Duck breast with cherries. Cherry in the glass meets a cherry sauce, and the acidity trims the richness of duck.
    Recipe idea: Score the duck skin, cook skin-side down 8 minutes, flip for 3 and rest; deglaze the pan with stock, add pitted cherries and reduce to a glossy sauce.
  5. Mushroom and thyme pasta. Earthy, savoury notes in the wine echo sautéed mushrooms and thyme.
    Recipe idea: Sauté sliced mushrooms with garlic and thyme in butter, toss with tagliatelle, a splash of cream and grated parmesan.
