About this wine

Frontera is made by Concha y Toro, one of Chile's largest wine producers, founded in the 1880s by Don Melchor Concha y Toro. Frontera means border in Spanish and refers to the natural barriers around Chile's vineyards: the Atacama Desert, the Andes, the Pacific Ocean and the glaciers of the south. This Cabernet Sauvignon comes from the Central Valley, the wide stretch south of Santiago that runs from Maipo down to Maule and gives most of Chile's wine. It rests for a short time in stainless steel tanks and cement vats, which keeps the fruit fresh and the style easy to drink.

Bottle sizes made: 250 ml, 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Stobá di kabritu. Medium body and soft tannins carry the spiced goat stew without weighing it down.
    Recipe idea: Marinate goat with lime, garlic and curry, brown it, add tomato, onion and sweet pepper and simmer slowly until tender.
  2. Pastechi. The blackcurrant fruit freshens the fried pastry and its savoury spiced beef filling.
    Recipe idea: Fill rounds of dough with ground beef cooked with onion, sweet pepper and raisins, fold, seal with a fork and fry until golden.
  3. Grilled pork chops. Juicy pork with a light char suits the easy tannins and dark fruit.
    Recipe idea: Rub chops with garlic, paprika, salt and oil, grill 5 minutes per side and rest a few minutes before serving.
  4. Herb roast lamb. Lamb with garlic and herbs matches the cassis and fresh herbal notes of the wine.
    Recipe idea: Rub a lamb leg with garlic, rosemary, thyme, salt and oil and roast at 180 C for about 90 minutes, then rest before slicing.
  5. Bean and vegetable chili. Mild spice and beans need a fruity red with soft tannins, not a heavy one.
    Recipe idea: Fry onion, garlic and peppers, add kidney beans, tomato, cumin and a little chili powder and simmer 30 minutes. Serve with rice.
