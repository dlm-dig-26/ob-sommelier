About this wine

Casillero del Diablo comes from Concha y Toro in Chile. The name means the Devil's Cellar and goes back more than a century: Don Melchor de Concha y Toro set aside a private stock of his best wines and spread the rumour that the devil lived in that cellar to keep people away. This Cabernet Sauvignon belongs to the Reserva range, made from grapes grown in Chile's Central Valley, with Marcelo Papa named as winemaker. It is full and smooth, with cassis and black cherry plus hints of coffee and dark chocolate. Serve it at 18 to 20 C.

On the shelf: Look for the small devil figure on the label, a nod to the Devil's Cellar legend behind the name.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Stobá di kabritu. Full body and dark fruit hold their own against spiced, slow-cooked goat.
    Recipe idea: Marinate goat with lime, garlic and cumin, brown it, then stew with onion, tomato, sweet pepper and a little wine for 2 hours.
  2. Carni stoba. Coffee and dark chocolate notes echo the deep, browned flavours of beef stew.
    Recipe idea: Brown beef chunks, add onion, garlic, tomato, sweet pepper, cumin and broth. Simmer covered 1.5 to 2 hours until tender.
  3. Grilled steak with chimichurri. Cassis and firm structure suit charred beef, and the herbs pick up the wine's freshness.
    Recipe idea: Grill a seasoned steak to taste. Blend parsley, garlic, oregano, vinegar, olive oil and chili flakes and spoon over the sliced meat.
  4. Rosemary lamb chops. Herby lamb is a classic match for Cabernet's black fruit and grip.
    Recipe idea: Rub lamb chops with olive oil, garlic, rosemary, salt and pepper. Grill or pan-sear 3 to 4 minutes per side.
  5. Aged cheese plate. Mature cheeses, as the producer suggests, soften the tannins and bring out the fruit.
    Recipe idea: Serve aged Gouda and mature cheddar at room temperature with crackers and a few walnuts.
