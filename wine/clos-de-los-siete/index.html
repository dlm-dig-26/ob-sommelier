About this wine

Clos de los Siete is a joint project in Vista Flores, Tunuyán, in the Uco Valley, led by the French winemaker Michel Rolland. In 1999 he invited six Bordeaux wine families to plant a large estate on untouched land. Today four wineries there, Rolland, Monteviejo, DiamAndes and Cuvelier Los Andes, each supply wine for this shared blend. Every winery ferments its small plots separately, and Rolland tastes dozens of tank and barrel samples before setting the final blend. Malbec leads, joined by Merlot, Syrah, Cabernet Sauvignon and small amounts of Petit Verdot and Cabernet Franc. The first vintage was 2002.

On the shelf: Look for the large star on the front label and the words Valle de Uco.

What to eat with it

  1. Stobá di kabritu. A full, layered blend has the structure for slow-cooked, spiced goat.
    Recipe idea: Marinate goat with lime, garlic and curry, brown it, add tomato, onion and sweet pepper and simmer slowly until soft.
  2. Carni stoba. The ripe dark fruit and fine tannins sit well with a rich beef stew.
    Recipe idea: Brown beef cubes, add onion, garlic, sweet pepper, tomato and cumin, then simmer covered for two hours until tender.
  3. Grilled steak. The Malbec and Cabernet in the blend love the char and fat of a grilled steak.
    Recipe idea: Season a sirloin with coarse salt, grill over high heat 4 minutes per side, rest 5 minutes and serve with chimichurri.
  4. Roast leg of lamb. Herbs and lamb match the tobacco and dark fruit notes of the blend.
    Recipe idea: Stud a leg of lamb with garlic and rosemary, rub with olive oil and roast at 180 C for about 90 minutes, then rest before carving.
  5. Mushroom risotto. Earthy mushrooms and parmesan bring out the savoury, toasty side of the wine.
    Recipe idea: Toast arborio rice in butter, add sautéed mushrooms, stir in hot stock little by little and finish with parmesan.
