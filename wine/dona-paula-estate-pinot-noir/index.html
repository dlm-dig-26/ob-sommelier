About this wine

Doña Paula was founded in Mendoza in 1997 by Ricardo Claro, then owner of Chile's Viña Santa Rita, and it is still part of the Santa Rita group. The name honours Paula Jaraquemada, a heroine of the independence era who sheltered soldiers. The Estate range is made only from the winery's own vineyards, and this Pinot Noir comes entirely from the Alluvia vineyard in Gualtallary, Tupungato, the highest of its four estates at around 1,350 metres in the Uco Valley. Cool mountain nights there help Pinot Noir keep fresh acidity and bright red fruit.

What to eat with it

  1. Grilled red snapper. Light tannins and bright acidity suit a firm, meaty fish without overpowering it.
    Recipe idea: Score a whole snapper, rub it with garlic, lime and thyme, then grill 6 to 8 minutes per side until the flesh flakes.
  2. Galinja stoba. Soft red fruit and fresh acidity match the gentle spice and savoury sauce of Aruban stewed chicken.
    Recipe idea: Brown chicken pieces, add onion, sweet pepper, tomato, a little cumin and soy sauce, then simmer covered for about 40 minutes.
  3. Keshi yena. Fresh acidity cuts through the melted cheese, and the soft tannins stay friendly with the spiced chicken.
    Recipe idea: Line a baking dish with Gouda slices, fill with spiced chicken, olives and raisins, cover with more cheese and bake until bubbling.
  4. Mushroom risotto. Earthy mushrooms bring out the savoury side of Pinot Noir, while its acidity keeps the creamy rice lively.
    Recipe idea: Sauté mushrooms and onion, toast arborio rice, add hot stock a ladle at a time, then finish with butter and parmesan.
  5. Roast pork tenderloin. Lean pork and red fruit flavours are a classic match for a light, fresh red.
    Recipe idea: Rub the tenderloin with garlic, rosemary and mustard, sear it, then roast at 200 °C for about 20 minutes and rest before slicing.
