About this wine

Casillero del Diablo is the best-known range of Concha y Toro, the Chilean wine company founded in 1883 by Don Melchor de Santiago Concha y Toro and today the largest wine producer and exporter in Latin America. The name means the Devil's Cellar: legend says Don Melchor kept his best wines for himself and spread the rumour that the Devil lived in his cellar to keep thieves away. This Sauvignon Blanc carries the Central Valley name; the producer also points to vineyards close to the Pacific, in Casablanca and Limarí, where sea breezes keep the grapes fresh. It is light and zesty, with peach, citrus and gooseberry.

Bottle sizes made: 187 ml, 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Ceviche. Zesty acidity and lime notes echo the citrus marinade and keep the raw fish bright.
    Recipe idea: Dice very fresh white fish, cover with lime juice, add red onion, chili, coriander and salt, rest 15 minutes and serve cold.
  2. Pisca hasa. A light, crisp white that refreshes after crispy fried fish, especially with a squeeze of lime.
    Recipe idea: Season fish fillets with lime, salt and garlic, dust with flour and fry until golden; serve with lime wedges and a fresh salad.
  3. Shrimp salad. Peach and citrus fruit match sweet shrimp, and the acidity cuts through a creamy dressing.
    Recipe idea: Toss cooked shrimp with avocado, cucumber, lettuce and a dressing of yogurt, lime and dill.
  4. Goat cheese salad. Sauvignon Blanc and fresh goat cheese are a classic: the tangy cheese meets a crisp, herbal wine.
    Recipe idea: Top mixed leaves with crumbled goat cheese, toasted nuts and sliced apple, and dress with olive oil and lemon.
  5. Cheese quiche. Lively acidity freshens the rich egg and cheese filling.
    Recipe idea: Line a tin with pastry, fill with eggs beaten with cream, grated cheese and softened onion, and bake 35 minutes at 180°C.
