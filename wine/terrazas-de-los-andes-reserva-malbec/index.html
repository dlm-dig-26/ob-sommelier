About this wine

Terrazas de los Andes grows its grapes on terraced plateaus in the foothills of the Andes. For the Reserva Malbec, fruit comes from vineyards between roughly 800 and 1,800 metres in Luján de Cuyo and the Uco Valley, from vines between 20 and 80 years old. The wine then spends 12 to 14 months in French oak barrels that are one to four years old, so the oak supports the fruit instead of dominating it. The result is a classic Mendoza Malbec: dark cherry, blackcurrant and blueberry, touches of cedar and vanilla, fine-grained tannins and a long, savoury finish.

What to eat with it

  1. Keshi yena. Velvety tannins and fresh acidity cut through melted gouda, and black cherry suits the spiced filling.
    Recipe idea: Line a baking dish with gouda slices, fill with chicken stewed with tomato, olives, raisins and capers, cover with cheese and bake at 180 C for 30 minutes.
  2. Stobá di kabritu. Plum, spice and chocolate notes match a fragrant goat stew.
    Recipe idea: Brown goat pieces, add onion, garlic, sweet pepper, tomato, cumin and a little hot pepper. Simmer covered 2 hours until tender.
  3. Grilled flank steak with chimichurri. A high-altitude Malbec's freshness balances the herby, garlicky sauce and charred beef.
    Recipe idea: Grill flank steak 4 minutes per side, rest and slice against the grain. Top with parsley, garlic, oregano, vinegar and olive oil.
  4. Sausage and mushroom pizza. Juicy dark fruit and moderate tannins suit savoury, cheesy pizza.
    Recipe idea: Top pizza dough with tomato sauce, mozzarella, sliced Italian sausage and mushrooms. Bake at the oven's highest setting for 10 to 12 minutes.
  5. Braised short ribs. Violet and vanilla notes and a velvety texture go with rich, slow-cooked beef.
    Recipe idea: Brown short ribs, add onion, carrot, garlic, tomato paste and a cup of red wine and stock. Braise covered at 160 C for 3 hours.
