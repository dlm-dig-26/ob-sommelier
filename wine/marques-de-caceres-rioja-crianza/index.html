About this wine

Marqués de Cáceres was founded in 1970 by Enrique Forner in Cenicero, in Rioja Alta, with the Bordeaux oenologist Émile Peynaud guiding its style from the start. Cristina Forner has led the family winery since 2007. The Crianza is mostly Tempranillo with small amounts of Garnacha and Graciano from selected plots in Rioja Alta and Rioja Alavesa, harvested by hand. It ages 12 months in French and American oak barrels, then rests at least a year in bottle before release.

On the shelf: Bordeaux shaped bottle.

Bottle sizes made: 375 ml, 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Stobá di kabritu. Rioja's red fruit, leather and sweet spice suit slow cooked goat with cumin without clashing.
    Recipe idea: Brown goat pieces, add onion, garlic, tomato, cumin, a little hot pepper and stock, then simmer covered about 2 hours.
  2. Galinja stoba. A medium bodied, round red with soft tannins works well with stewed chicken in tomato sauce.
    Recipe idea: Brown chicken pieces, add onion, sweet pepper, garlic, tomato, olives and a dash of soy, cover with stock and simmer 45 minutes.
  3. Lamb chops with rosemary. Lamb is a Rioja classic; the vanilla and spice from oak echo the herbs and char.
    Recipe idea: Rub lamb chops with olive oil, garlic and rosemary, grill 3 minutes per side and finish with sea salt.
  4. Spanish chorizo and potatoes. Smoky paprika and rich pork pair with the wine's plum fruit and rounded tannins.
    Recipe idea: Fry sliced chorizo, add cubed potatoes, onion and a pinch of smoked paprika, cook until tender and golden.
  5. Manchego and serrano ham. Firm sheep cheese and cured ham bring out the wine's ripe fruit and balanced finish.
    Recipe idea: Slice aged Manchego and serrano ham, serve with almonds, olives and toasted bread rubbed with tomato.
