About this wine

Alamos belongs to the Catena Zapata group. The Catena family has farmed vineyards in Mendoza since 1902 and is now in its fifth generation. The Alamos winery is in Vista Flores, Tunuyán, and its winemaker is Lucía Vaieretti. Alamos Malbec is a hand-harvested, dry Malbec from Mendoza with dark cherry, blackberry, plum, violet, spice and vanilla. Most sources describe it as medium to full-bodied with soft, round tannins, which makes it an easy first taste of the signature grape of Argentina.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml, 1,5 L. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Grilled ribeye. Malbec and grilled beef are the classic Argentine pairing; the fruit loves the char.
    Recipe idea: Season a ribeye with salt and pepper, grill over high heat 4 minutes per side, rest 5 minutes and serve with chimichurri.
  2. Stobá di kabritu. Plum and spice in the wine match the cumin and pepper in the goat stew.
    Recipe idea: Brown goat pieces, add onion, garlic, tomato, cumin and a little hot pepper, cover with stock and simmer 2 hours until tender.
  3. Pastechi with beef. Soft, round tannins suit the spiced beef filling and crisp pastry.
    Recipe idea: Fill pastry rounds with spiced minced beef, raisins and olives, fold into half-moons, seal and deep-fry until golden.
  4. Beef empanadas. A Mendoza Malbec with an Argentine classic is a natural fit.
    Recipe idea: Fill pastry rounds with beef fried with onion, cumin and paprika, fold, seal and bake at 200°C for about 20 minutes.
  5. Barbecue ribs. Ripe dark fruit and a touch of vanilla echo a smoky, sweet glaze.
    Recipe idea: Rub pork ribs with paprika, garlic and brown sugar, bake covered at 150°C for 2 hours, then glaze and grill.
