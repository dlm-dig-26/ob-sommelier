About this wine

This Merlot comes from Laureano Gomez, a small family winery in Colonia Las Rosas, Tunuyán, in the Uco Valley of Mendoza. The winery carries the name of its enologist, and Merlot is one of its signature grapes, next to Malbec. In the high, cool Uco Valley, Merlot ripens slowly and keeps its freshness, which typically gives a medium-bodied, dry red with plum and red cherry fruit, a hint of spice and smooth tannins. Visitors can taste at the winery, where a family restaurant serves lunch.

What to eat with it

  1. Galinja stoba. Chicken stewed with tomato and peppers suits a medium red with soft tannins and plum fruit.
    Recipe idea: Brown chicken pieces, add onion, garlic, sweet pepper, tomato paste and a little soy sauce, then simmer 45 minutes until tender.
  2. Pastechi. Fried pastries with spiced beef are a fun match for a juicy red that is not too heavy.
    Recipe idea: Fill rounds of dough with spiced ground beef, raisins and olives, fold, seal with a fork and fry until golden.
  3. Roast pork loin. Pork is lean enough for moderate tannins and gains from the ripe fruit.
    Recipe idea: Rub a pork loin with garlic, thyme and paprika, roast at 180 C for about an hour, rest and slice.
  4. Mushroom pasta. Earthy mushrooms bring out the savoury side of Merlot.
    Recipe idea: Sauté mushrooms with garlic and thyme, add a splash of cream and some parmesan and toss with tagliatelle.
  5. Spaghetti bolognese. Tomato and minced meat match the acidity and body of the wine.
    Recipe idea: Brown minced beef with onion, carrot and celery, add tomato, simmer 40 minutes and serve over spaghetti.
