About this wine

689 Cellars was founded by Curtis McBride together with Napa winemaker Kent Rasmussen. The name comes from the numbers 6, 8 and 9, which stand for happiness, prosperity and long life, an idea the founders picked up on their travels in Asia. The grapes come from Napa Valley, and the blend has included Zinfandel, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Petite Sirah, with the exact mix changing from vintage to vintage. After time in oak barrels it is smooth and velvety, with ripe dark berries, sweet spice and dark chocolate.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Carni stoba. Velvety dark fruit and sweet spice suit a slow-cooked, cumin-scented beef stew.
    Recipe idea: Brown beef cubes, add onion, garlic, sweet pepper, tomato and cumin, pour in a splash of wine and simmer covered for about 2 hours.
  2. Stobá di kabritu. Enough body and spice to match goat stewed with tomato and pepper.
    Recipe idea: Marinate goat in lime, garlic and pepper, brown it, then simmer with onion, tomato, cumin and a little Scotch bonnet for 2 to 3 hours.
  3. Barbecue ribs. Ripe berries and a hint of chocolate pair well with a sweet, smoky sauce.
    Recipe idea: Rub pork ribs with paprika, brown sugar and salt, bake wrapped in foil at low heat for 2 hours, then glaze with barbecue sauce on the grill.
  4. Grilled steak. Smooth tannins and dark fruit flatter a juicy, charred steak.
    Recipe idea: Season a ribeye with salt, pepper and garlic, grill over high heat 4 minutes per side and let it rest 5 minutes before slicing.
  5. Mushroom risotto. Earthy mushrooms bring out the savoury, spicy side of the blend.
    Recipe idea: Toast arborio rice in butter with onion, add sautéed mushrooms and hot stock ladle by ladle for 18 minutes, finish with Parmesan.
