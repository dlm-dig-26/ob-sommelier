About this wine

Moët Impérial is the signature non-vintage Champagne of Moët & Chandon. It was first released in 1869 as Brut Impérial, a name that honours the house's long ties with the French imperial family, and its first shipment was timed to the centenary of Napoleon Bonaparte's birth. The wine is assembled from more than 100 different wines, blending Pinot Noir, Meunier and Chardonnay, with 20 to 30 percent reserve wines to keep the house style consistent from year to year. In 2019 it celebrated its 150th anniversary with a limited-edition bottle and a redesigned logo built around the letter I of Impérial.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml, 1,5 L. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Oysters on ice. Crisp acidity and mineral freshness match the briny bite of raw oysters.
    Recipe idea: Shuck chilled oysters and serve on crushed ice with lemon wedges and a mignonette of shallot and red wine vinegar.
  2. Fish ceviche. Lively citrus notes and fine bubbles echo lime-cured fish.
    Recipe idea: Cube fresh wahoo or snapper, cover with lime juice for 15 minutes, then add red onion, cilantro, a little hot pepper and salt.
  3. Keshi yena. Brioche notes and fresh acidity cut through melted Gouda and the savoury filling.
    Recipe idea: Line a buttered dish with Gouda slices, fill with stewed chicken, olives, raisins and capers, cover with more cheese and bake until golden.
  4. Fried calamari. Fine bubbles and lemony freshness cut the batter like a squeeze of citrus.
    Recipe idea: Slice squid into rings, dust in seasoned flour and fry briefly in hot oil until crisp. Serve with lemon and garlic mayonnaise.
  5. Roast chicken. Apple and pear fruit with a light body suit tender roast poultry.
    Recipe idea: Rub a whole chicken with butter, lemon zest, garlic and thyme and roast at 200 C for about 75 minutes, until the juices run clear.
