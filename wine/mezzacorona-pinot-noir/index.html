About this wine

Mezzacorona was founded in 1904 in the village of Mezzocorona in Trentino, in the far north of Italy. Its wines come from a community of member growers who farm vineyards in the hills and valleys of the region. This Pinot Noir carries the Vigneti delle Dolomiti designation, for vineyards in the foothills of the Dolomite mountains. Pinot Noir is one of the most demanding grapes to grow, and a cool mountain climate helps it keep fresh acidity and delicate red fruit. The result is a light, fruity red that is easy to pair with food.

What to eat with it

  1. Grilled red snapper. A light, low tannin red with fresh acidity works with meaty grilled fish without any metallic taste.
    Recipe idea: Score a whole snapper, rub with garlic, lime, salt and pepper, grill 6 to 8 minutes per side and serve with lime wedges.
  2. Pisca hasa. Bright red cherry and crisp acidity cut through crisp fried fish and its creole sauce.
    Recipe idea: Season fish fillets with lime, garlic and salt, dust with flour and fry until golden. Serve with a quick sauce of onion, sweet pepper and tomato.
  3. Grilled salmon. Salmon is rich enough for a red, and Pinot Noir is the classic light partner.
    Recipe idea: Brush salmon fillets with olive oil, season with salt, pepper and dill, grill skin side down 4 minutes, turn and cook 2 minutes more.
  4. Mushroom risotto. Earthy mushrooms bring out the soft, savoury side of Pinot Noir.
    Recipe idea: Soften onion in butter, toast arborio rice, add stock little by little, stir in sautéed mushrooms and finish with parmesan.
  5. Roast chicken. Tender chicken needs a gentle red with fresh fruit, not heavy tannins.
    Recipe idea: Rub a whole chicken with butter, garlic, thyme and lemon, roast at 200 C for about 75 minutes and rest before carving.
