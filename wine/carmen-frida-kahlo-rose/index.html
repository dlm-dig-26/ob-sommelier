About this wine

Viña Carmen, founded in 1850, calls itself Chile's first winery and is part of the Santa Rita group. Its Frida Kahlo rosé was created by the Santa Rita team as a limited edition inspired by the painter's passion, elegance and attitude, and it is sold in both bottles and cans. The packaging was designed by Dizen, a studio in Mendoza, Argentina. The wine comes from Chile's Central Valley and is a dry blend of red grapes; in the 2023 vintage, Syrah and Grenache led the mix. Serve it chilled as an aperitif or with light dishes.

On the shelf: Limited-edition label with the Frida Kahlo name and Carmen branding; the same wine also comes in a can.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Ceviche. Brisk acidity matches lime-cured fish, and the red-berry fruit softens the chili.
    Recipe idea: Cube fresh white fish, cover with lime juice for 15 minutes, then add red onion, chili, cilantro and salt. Serve cold.
  2. Pastechi. A crisp, dry rosé refreshes the palate between bites of fried pastry filled with cheese or meat.
    Recipe idea: Roll out dough, fill with Gouda or spiced minced beef, fold into half-moons, seal the edges and fry until golden.
  3. Grilled red snapper. Light body and a salty, mineral touch suit delicate grilled fish with herbs.
    Recipe idea: Score a whole snapper, rub with garlic, lime, salt and pepper, grill 6 to 8 minutes per side and serve with a Creole sauce.
  4. Strawberry and goat cheese salad. Strawberry and melon notes echo the fruit, and the acidity cuts the creamy cheese.
    Recipe idea: Toss mixed greens with sliced strawberries, crumbled goat cheese and toasted almonds; dress with olive oil and balsamic vinegar.
  5. Margherita pizza. Fresh acidity balances the tomato, and the light body leaves room for mozzarella and basil.
    Recipe idea: Spread crushed tomato on pizza dough, add salt, olive oil and mozzarella, bake in a very hot oven for 8 to 10 minutes and finish with basil.
