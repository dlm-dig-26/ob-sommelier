About this wine

Rutini Wines goes back to 1885, when Italian-born Felipe Rutini founded Bodega La Rural near Mendoza. The family planted its first vines in the Uco Valley in 1925, and the wines are now made in a modern winery in Tupungato, with Mariano Di Paola as head winemaker. This Malbec comes from a single vineyard in Gualtallary, a high area of Tupungato in the Uco Valley where Rutini owns vineyards. It spends about 12 months in a mix of new and used French oak barrels. Expect fresh blackberry, plum and violet with graphite and herbal notes, bright acidity and fine tannins.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Grilled beef tenderloin. Fine tannins and fresh dark fruit suit lean, tender beef from the grill.
    Recipe idea: Season a whole tenderloin with salt and pepper, sear on all sides, finish over indirect heat until pink inside and rest before slicing.
  2. Stobá di kabritu. Bright acidity and structure stand up to slow-cooked goat with cumin and pepper.
    Recipe idea: Brown goat pieces with onion, garlic and cumin, add tomato, sweet pepper and a little hot pepper, cover and simmer two hours until tender.
  3. Carni stoba. Plum and violet notes match a tomato-rich beef stew, and the acidity keeps it lively.
    Recipe idea: Brown beef cubes, add onion, tomato, peppers, capers and olives, cover with stock and simmer 90 minutes; serve with rice or funchi.
  4. Roast lamb with rosemary. The herbal and graphite notes echo rosemary and garlic on roast lamb.
    Recipe idea: Rub a leg of lamb with garlic, rosemary, olive oil and salt, roast at 180°C until pink inside and rest before carving.
  5. Mushroom and beef burger. Earthy mushrooms and juicy beef suit a fresh, structured Malbec.
    Recipe idea: Grill a seasoned beef patty, top with mushrooms sautéed in garlic and a slice of cheese, and serve on a toasted bun.
