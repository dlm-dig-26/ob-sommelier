About this wine

Wapisa belongs to Fincas Patagónicas, the group of wineries led by owner and president Patricia Ortiz, which also includes Tapiz and Zolo in Mendoza. Wapisa grows its grapes in San Javier, in the Río Negro province of Patagonia, and calls itself the only winery on the Patagonian Atlantic coast. Its vineyard of about 60 hectares lies in a young wine area close to where the Río Negro meets the sea, which is why the winery describes its wines as maritime. The name Wapisa is said to mean whale in a local indigenous language.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Stobá di kabritu. Firm tannins and dark fruit match the strong flavour of goat slowly stewed with spices.
    Recipe idea: Brown goat pieces, then simmer slowly with onion, garlic, tomato, sweet pepper, cumin and a splash of vinegar until tender. Serve with funchi or rice.
  2. Carni stoba. A full, ripe Cabernet with tobacco and spice suits a hearty beef stew.
    Recipe idea: Brown beef cubes, add onion, tomato, bell pepper, capers and olives, cover with stock and simmer for about two hours. Serve with white rice.
  3. Grilled ribeye. The tannins cut through the marbled fat, and the blackcurrant meets the smoky char.
    Recipe idea: Rub a thick ribeye with salt, pepper and a little oil, grill over high heat 5 minutes per side, rest, then slice and serve with chimichurri.
  4. Lamb chops with rosemary. Herbal, spicy notes in the wine echo the rosemary and garlic on grilled lamb.
    Recipe idea: Marinate lamb chops in olive oil, garlic, rosemary and lemon zest for an hour, then grill 3 to 4 minutes per side.
  5. Aged Gouda. The firm structure and dark chocolate finish stand up to a salty, nutty aged cheese.
    Recipe idea: Cut aged Gouda into chunks and serve with walnuts, dried figs and dark bread.
