About this wine

Proyecto Las Compuertas is the project of the Durigutti brothers in Las Compuertas, Luján de Cuyo. It began in 2007, when they bought their first five hectares of historic Malbec vineyards; the estate, now 34 hectares, is called Finca Victoria after their mother. This Malbec is named for the year its vines were planted, 1914, and grows on the clay soils typical of Las Compuertas. The grapes ferment in egg shaped concrete tanks, then the wine rests about five months in a 2,000 litre oak cask, so fruit and floral notes stay in front.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Stobá di kabritu. Fine tannins and plum fruit stand up to slow cooked goat with cumin without overpowering it.
    Recipe idea: Brown goat pieces, add onion, garlic, tomato, cumin and a little Madame Jeanette pepper, cover with stock and simmer about 2 hours.
  2. Keshi yena. Fresh acidity cuts the melted Gouda while cherry and violet notes lift the spiced filling.
    Recipe idea: Line a dish with Gouda slices, fill with chicken stewed with onion, tomato, olives, raisins and capers, top with cheese and bake 30 minutes.
  3. Grilled flank steak with chimichurri. A classic Argentine match: the herbs echo the wine's freshness and the beef softens its mineral grip.
    Recipe idea: Grill flank steak to medium rare, rest it, slice across the grain and spoon over parsley, garlic, oregano, vinegar and olive oil.
  4. Duck breast with cherry sauce. Black cherry fruit mirrors the sauce and the wine's acidity balances the rich skin.
    Recipe idea: Score and sear duck skin side down, finish in the oven, then deglaze the pan with cherries, a splash of stock and a spoon of honey.
  5. Grilled portobello burgers. Earthy, meaty mushrooms suit the graphite side of the wine and its medium tannins.
    Recipe idea: Marinate portobellos in garlic, soy and olive oil, grill 4 minutes per side and serve in buns with grilled onions and arugula.
