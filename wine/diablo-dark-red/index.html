About this wine

Diablo Dark Red comes from Concha y Toro, the Chilean wine company based in Santiago, and is made by winemaker Héctor Urzúa. It is a blend led by Syrah and Malbec, with some Merlot in recent vintages, from the Maule Valley in central Chile. After harvest the grapes ferment for about ten days in stainless steel tanks, then the wine rests in American and French oak barrels of different ages and toast levels. That oak adds soft vanilla, mocha and caramel touches on top of ripe dark fruit, for a smooth, full red that is ready to drink young.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Stobá di kabritu. Ripe fruit and a touch of softness stand up to the warm spices of goat stew.
    Recipe idea: Marinate goat with lime, garlic and curry, brown it, add tomato, onion, sweet pepper and a little hot pepper and simmer slowly until tender.
  2. Carni stoba. A full, velvety red matches the deep, slow cooked flavour of Aruban beef stew.
    Recipe idea: Brown beef cubes, add onion, garlic, sweet pepper, tomato, cumin and a splash of wine and simmer covered for two hours.
  3. Barbecue ribs. The sweet, smoky glaze echoes the vanilla and caramel notes from the oak.
    Recipe idea: Rub pork ribs with paprika, brown sugar, garlic and salt, bake covered at 150 C for 2 hours, brush with barbecue sauce and grill until sticky.
  4. Beef chili. Soft tannins and ripe fruit handle gentle heat without turning bitter.
    Recipe idea: Brown ground beef with onion and garlic, add kidney beans, tomato, cumin and chili powder and simmer 40 minutes. Serve with rice or tortilla chips.
  5. Pulled pork sandwich. Rich, slightly sweet pork goes well with a plush red that has a chocolatey finish.
    Recipe idea: Slow cook pork shoulder with onion, garlic, paprika and a little vinegar until it falls apart, shred, mix with barbecue sauce and serve on soft buns.
