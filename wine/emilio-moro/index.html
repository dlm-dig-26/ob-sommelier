About this wine

This is the flagship red of Bodegas Emilio Moro and carries the name of the man who shaped the winery. According to a Dutch retailer, the family has grown vines in Pesquera de Duero, Ribera del Duero, since 1892 and now farms about 200 hectares. The wine is Tempranillo, known locally as Tinto Fino, from vines around 25 to 35 years old. It is fermented in temperature-controlled steel tanks and then aged for about a year in French and American oak, giving dark fruit, coffee and spice.

On the shelf: Look for the round picture medallion on the label: Emilio Moro harvesting with his father in the La Rendeja vineyard, 1938.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml, 1,5 L. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Stobá di kabritu. Firm tannins and dark fruit stand up to rich, spiced goat stew.
    Recipe idea: Marinate goat with lime, garlic and cumin, brown it, then simmer with onion, tomato, sweet pepper and a little hot pepper until tender.
  2. Carni stoba. A full, structured red matches the depth of slow-cooked beef.
    Recipe idea: Brown beef chunks, add onion, garlic, tomato, sweet pepper and a splash of vinegar, then stew gently for two hours.
  3. Roast lamb. Classic Ribera match: the oak spice and tannin love roasted lamb.
    Recipe idea: Rub a leg of lamb with garlic, rosemary, olive oil and salt and roast until pink inside; rest before slicing.
  4. Grilled ribeye. Charred, fatty steak softens the tannins and brings out the fruit.
    Recipe idea: Season a thick ribeye with salt and pepper, grill over high heat a few minutes per side and rest five minutes.
  5. Aged cheese board. Hard, nutty cheeses suit the wine's coffee and spice notes.
    Recipe idea: Serve aged Manchego and old Gouda with walnuts, quince paste and slices of bread.
