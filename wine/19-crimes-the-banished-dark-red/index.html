About this wine

19 Crimes is an Australian brand from Treasury Wine Estates, created in 2012 and themed on British convicts who were sent to Australia. The Banished is linked to James Wilson, a convict court-martialled for desertion and mutinous conduct, whose death sentence became life with hard labour before he was shipped from Ireland. The wine is mostly Shiraz with Cabernet Sauvignon and Grenache from South Eastern Australia, aged three months in American oak. It is rich and plush, off-dry, with blackberry, plum, dark chocolate, vanilla and baking spice.

Bottle sizes made: 750 ml. Stores in Aruba may carry only some of them.

What to eat with it

  1. Carni stoba. Plush dark fruit and soft sweetness round out a rich, spiced beef stew.
    Recipe idea: Brown beef cubes, add onion, garlic, sweet pepper, tomato and cumin, pour in a splash of wine and simmer covered for about 2 hours.
  2. Stobá di kabritu. Baking spice and vanilla from the oak echo the cumin and warmth of goat stew.
    Recipe idea: Marinate goat in lime, garlic and pepper, brown it, then simmer with onion, tomato, cumin and a little Scotch bonnet for 2 to 3 hours.
  3. Grilled steak. Ripe Shiraz fruit and medium tannins suit charred, juicy beef.
    Recipe idea: Season a ribeye with salt, pepper and garlic, grill over high heat 4 minutes per side and let it rest 5 minutes before slicing.
  4. Barbecue ribs. A touch of sweetness matches a sweet and smoky barbecue sauce.
    Recipe idea: Rub pork ribs with paprika, brown sugar and salt, bake wrapped in foil at low heat for 2 hours, then glaze with barbecue sauce on the grill.
  5. Chocolate brownies. Dark chocolate and mocha notes in the wine meet a not-too-sweet brownie.
    Recipe idea: Melt dark chocolate with butter, stir in sugar, eggs and a little flour, and bake at 175°C for about 25 minutes until just set.
